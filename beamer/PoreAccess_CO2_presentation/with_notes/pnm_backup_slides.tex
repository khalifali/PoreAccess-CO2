\begin{frame}{1. Particle positions define candidate pore nodes}
\begin{columns}[c,onlytextwidth]
\column{0.47\textwidth}\centering
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=1.0]
\coordinate (a) at (0,0);\coordinate (b) at (3.4,0.3);\coordinate (c) at (1.0,1.4);\coordinate (d) at (1.8,3.3);
\draw[tumblue,thick] (a)--(b)--(d)--cycle;
\draw[tumblue,dashed] (a)--(c)--(b) (c)--(d);
\foreach \p/\n in {a/1,b/2,c/3,d/4}{\filldraw[fill=tumlight,draw=tumblue] (\p) circle (0.36);\node at (\p) {$\n$};}
\node[circle,fill=tumorange,inner sep=3pt] (p) at (1.55,1.25) {};
\node[anchor=west,text=tumdark] at (2.1,1.75) {pore node};\draw[tumdark] (2.1,1.65)--(p);
\node[align=center,font=\footnotesize,text=tumgray] at (1.7,-0.8) {Schematic 3D projection\\Bead centres form a tetrahedron};
\end{tikzpicture}
\column{0.49\textwidth}\small
\begin{itemize}
\item Start with known bead centres, radii and bed boundaries.
\item Delaunay tetrahedralization groups neighbouring centres into sets of four.
\item Locate the circumcentre: the point equidistant from those four centres.
\item Retain valid points inside the bed and outside the solid as pore nodes.
\end{itemize}
\medskip
A node represents a gas region. Its volume is assigned later.
\end{columns}
\result{The particle geometry determines where the pore nodes are placed.}
\note{Our beads have equal radii, so ordinary Delaunay geometry is used. The sketch is a projected tetrahedron. Its drawn pore marker is illustrative. The actual code computes each circumcentre in three dimensions, rejects singular tetrahedra and checks the point against the bed domain and bead surfaces. A pore node is a representative location rather than a reconstructed cavity surface.}
\end{frame}

\begin{columns}[c,onlytextwidth]
\column{0.47\textwidth}\centering
\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=1.05]

\draw[tumorange,line width=2pt] (0.9,1.3)--(2.8,1.3);
\foreach \x/\lab in {0.9/i,2.8/j}{\node[circle,fill=tumorange,inner sep=3pt,label=below:{$\lab$}] at (\x,1.3) {};}
\foreach \v in {a,b,c,l,r}{\filldraw[fill=tumlight,draw=tumblue] (\v) circle (0.18);}
\node[font=\footnotesize,align=center,text=tumgray] at (1.8,-0.7) {Two tetrahedra share three beads\\Schematic 3D projection};
\end{tikzpicture}
\column{0.49\textwidth}\small
\begin{itemize}
\item Two neighbouring tetrahedra share a triangular face.
\item The three beads around that face define an opening.
\item A valid opening connects pore nodes $i$ and $j$ through a throat.
\item Estimate throat length $L_{ij}$ and circular area $A_{ij}$ from geometry.
\end{itemize}
\[
G_{ij}=D_m\frac{A_{ij}}{L_{ij}}.
\]
\end{columns}
\result{At this stage we know the nodes and connections. Sobol sampling has not been used.}
\note{The code uses shared Delaunay faces to identify candidate throats. Length is the distance between pore nodes. The throat radius is approximated by the shared-face circumradius minus the bead radius, and the area by pi times radius squared. Closed or degenerate connections are rejected. This circular opening is a modelling approximation, not the exact three-sphere aperture.}
\end{frame}

\end{itemize}

V_i=V_{\rm void}\frac{N_i+0.5}{\sum_j(N_j+0.5)}.
\]

Normalization fixes the total but does not prove the local volumes are exact or boundary-conforming.}

\item Throats transfer gas according to concentration differences.
\item Associated beads adsorb gas from adjacent pores.
\item Boundary connections supply gas from the inlet reservoir.
\end{itemize}

This construction describes the gas space between beads; bead uptake kinetics represent the internal adsorption process.}
